The short versiooon

The problem

Corporate perks fail quietly. HR announces them once, a few people activate, nobody remembers. Perks also sounds like a scam: free money on things you already buy.

What I did

Designed the whole product around the Skeptic. A scroll story that earns belief before making a claim, a portal that stays out of the way, and a system that keeps every rupee honest.

What shipped

A marketing site, a 12-route portal with 71 static pages, an assistant that cannot invent a rate, a white-label system for three employers and a 13-chapter brand book.

01

The problem

The invisible benefiiit

A benefit only creates value if people use it. Perks is not an app you download because you want it. It's something you have to be told you have, then believe, then start, then remember. Four places to lose someone before a single rupee is earned.

  1. 1

    Told

    "I didn't know I had this." It arrives in an HR email between an insurance renewal and a fire drill.

    Ignorance
  2. 2

    Believe

    "What's the catch?" Free money on things you already buy sets off every scam alarm. The biggest drop-off.

    Disbelief
  3. 3

    Start

    "It's probably a whole thing." Any setup cost, a new app, a code, a different store, ends it.

    Friction
  4. 4

    Remember

    "Oh right, that thing." Without a reason to come back, the benefit becomes a line item nobody uses.

    Forgetting
How might we

make an invisible benefit feel like a gift you were already owed: credible enough to trust, effortless enough to start, and good enough to come back to?

02

Who it's for

Four people, one who matters most

I wrote four working personas instead of one average user. Three of them convert on desire. One converts only on proof, and that one shaped every screen.

03

Strategy

Earn the belief before the claaaim

"Make your Salary go further" is a slogan. I wanted it to be an answer. So before the site explains a single feature, a 47-second scroll film shows a salary landing and running out.

Salary credited₹50,000Payday.

Hover or tap the beats

Why a film first

It creates the gap before offering the fix. Every claim after it lands on a feeling that already exists: "I wish I could do more."

Why tap and hold

Browsers block sound until someone taps. Rather than apologise with a mute button, I turned that tap into the start: you light up your invite. It also makes the loading wait feel active.

How I kept it fair

  • A clear Skip intro and mute, always visible
  • Phones and reduced-motion get a light path, no film
  • Never replays after login or logout
  • A 9-second failsafe that frees the page if scripts never load

The voice

One trick: stretch the vowel

Once per line, on the key word. Sentence case, real numbers, no exclamation marks, no fake urgency. The voice does the anti-scam work a louder voice would undo.

04

Design system

Small rules, kept everywhere

A product that's mostly a number and a feeling needs few parts, used strictly. The whole system fits on one page and ships as a brand book with a "why we did it" on every chapter.

Two colours do the work

Coral#D74446Section heads, the yes

Navy#242C51Page heads, ink
Green#009D73Only for "with Perks"
Ground#F6F4F4Never pure white*

* In the product, an off-white ground hides hairline seams between stacked full-width sections. A third accent colour means something has gone wrong.

06

Performance is a design material

One measurement changed the plan

The scroll felt heavy. I ran the same scroll twice: once normally, once with reduced motion on.

Normal
26.4 ms
Reduced motion
15.2 ms

About 40% of every scroll frame was the motion layer, not layout. So the fix wasn't to redesign anything. It was to stop paying for effects that weren't on screen.

Average scroll frame
26.4ms23.2ms

Removed a blur that ran even at zero, and GPU layers held by off-screen sections.

Worst frame
192ms150ms

Paused off-screen video; stopped a clip downloading twice.

Initial transfer
52.0MB45.1MB

Deferred the closing video until it's near.

Top Brands art
8.9MB432KB

Sized for 3× on a 220px tile, and no bigger.

A judgement call: I removed the gyro tilt on phones. It was the last thing costing frames on the device where it lived. A delight that costs frames isn't a delight. The tilts and sheens stay, just still.
A trade I refused. A 14MB version of the reel video looked blurry on retina. 24MB stayed. Smaller isn't better if it looks cheap to the Skeptic.
